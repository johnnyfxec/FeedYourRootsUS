## 1.1 Brand Overview

**Brand Name:** Feed Your Roots
**Domain:** feedyourroots.us

### Tagline — 5 Options, Ranked

1. **"Half an acre. A whole life."** ← RECOMMENDED
2. "Grow what feeds you."
3. "The land is waiting."
4. "Small land. Full table."
5. "Plant today. Eat forever."

**Recommendation rationale:** Option 1 wins because it contains the brand's entire thesis in six words — it names the objection (small land) and the payoff (a whole life) in one breath. It's memorable, rhythmic, and works on a PDF cover, an Instagram bio, and a billboard equally well. Option 2 is the runner-up for CTAs and ad copy.

### Mission Statement

Feed Your Roots exists to prove that any family, on any patch of land, can grow real food, real skills, and real security. We turn half an acre into a working system of trees, seeds, bees, and harvests — teaching ordinary people that provision was never out of reach. It was under their feet.

### Brand Promise

Every Feed Your Roots resource gives you one thing you can plant, build, or preserve this week — no jargon, no gatekeeping, no six-figure homestead required.

### Brand Archetype

**Caregiver + Explorer hybrid.** The Caregiver side nurtures: it feeds families, protects the soil, keeps the seeds safe for next year. The Explorer side empowers: it hands you the map and says "you can do this yourself." The brand never rescues — it equips. Every piece of content should answer two questions at once: *Are you taking care of the reader?* and *Are you making them more capable, more free?*

## 1.2 Brand Story (Origin Narrative)

We didn't start with six acres. We started with a question: how much land does it actually take to feed a family?

The answer surprised us. Half an acre — planted with intention — can carry an orchard, a seed library, a hive, a flock, and a garden at the back door. Not someday. Not after you've "learned enough." Starting now, with the ground you already have.

We are gardeners, parents, beekeepers, and stubborn optimists who believe food security isn't a bunker or a bank account. It's a fruit tree planted on an ordinary Tuesday. It's seeds saved in a mason jar. It's a child who knows what a ripe tomato smells like.

The land is not asking you to be an expert. It's asking you to begin.

Come put your hands in the dirt with us.

### Illustration Style Guide (verbatim for AI image generators)

> Hand-drawn storybook illustration in the tradition of Beatrix Potter, modernized with the flat, confident color sensibility of contemporary Oatly packaging. Fine ink linework: delicate, slightly imperfect 1–2pt lines with visible hand tremor, precise on subjects, looser at edges. Color applied as soft watercolor washes that bleed gently past the linework, layered over a warm parchment background (#F5ECD7) — never pure white. Palette limited to forest green, terracotta orange, warm brown, harvest gold. Vegetables, bees, and animals may have subtle, kind faces — a dot eye, a small smile — never cartoonish, never Pixar-round, never anthropomorphized bodies with arms and legs. Compositions breathe: at least 30% negative parchment space, single focal subject or small vignette, optional thin hand-drawn border of leaves or vines. Texture: light paper grain, dry-brush edges, occasional pencil under-sketch showing through. NEVER include: photorealism, 3D rendering, neon or saturated primary colors, digital gradients, text or lettering, human faces, sci-fi or fantasy elements, plastic objects, hard black outlines, pure white backgrounds. Mood: quiet, warm, unhurried — a Sunday morning on land you love.

## 1.7 Content System for Claude Sonnet — Replication Prompt Templates
